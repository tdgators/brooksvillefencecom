Pool Fence Inspection Guide

Getting your pool barrier ready for a Hernando County inspection.

Get a Free Estimate Call 813-669-4555

Hernando County applies the same state pool barrier rules as the rest of Florida. Here's what to check before your inspection, whether you're in Spring Hill, Brooksville or out in the county.

Walk the barrier like an inspector

Everything below comes from Florida's pool barrier statute (Fla. Stat. 515.29). Your inspector also uses the Florida Building Code, which spells out exact openings and latch heights, so use this as a pre-check rather than a guarantee.

What trips people up in Hernando County

On sloped Brooksville lots, the fence that measures 4 feet on the high side can come up short on the low side, so we measure along the whole run. In Spring Hill, we often find older screen enclosures with doors that no longer close on their own; a new closer usually fixes it.

Height and gaps

The barrier has to be at least 4 feet tall measured from the outside. On rolling Brooksville lots, measure at the lowest point, not the highest. Then look along the bottom for washouts and along the face for footholds or openings a small child could use.

The gate

Open it a few inches and let go, then open it all the way and let go. Both times it should swing shut and latch on its own. It must open outward, away from the pool, and the latch release has to be on the pool side out of a child's reach.

Climbing aids, doors and distance

Clear the outside of the fence: no pumps, feeders, wheelbarrows or furniture a child could climb. If the house wall is part of the barrier, doors or windows opening onto the pool need an approved safety feature such as an exit alarm (Fla. Stat. 515.27). And the barrier must sit far enough from the water that someone who gets past it can't fall straight in.

Quick Facts

  • Measure height on the low side of the yard
  • Close the gate from every angle
  • Check under the fence after rain
  • Move anything a child could climb
  • Check house doors facing the pool
